You post the job. People apply. You set up interviews, and half of them don't come. Someone finally starts, works two nights and stops answering your texts. Then you're back on the night shift yourself.

If that sounds familiar, you're not doing anything unusual. Most cleaning company owners deal with it. The good news is that a lot of no-shows have the same few causes, and most of them can be fixed before you ever hire anyone.

Here's what we see work.

## Why cleaners don't show up

Before you change how you hire, it helps to know why people disappear. It's rarely because they're lazy. Most of the time, it's one of these:

- **The drive is too long.** A building that's 40 minutes away looks fine on the application. After a week of night shifts, it isn't.
- **The schedule doesn't fit.** Many good cleaners already have a day job or family duties. If your shift overlaps with either one, they'll leave the moment something gives.
- **The pay was a surprise.** If someone finds out the real pay in the interview, or on their first check, they stop trusting you.
- **Nobody called them back fast enough.** Good cleaners apply to several jobs at once. The first company to call usually gets them.
- **They didn't know what the job really was.** "Cleaner" can mean a quiet office or a busy medical building. People quit when the job isn't what they pictured.

Notice that almost all of these can be caught before someone's first day.

## Write the real job, not a generic ad

Your job post should answer the questions a cleaner will ask anyway. Put these in plain words, near the top:

- **Where:** the area or neighborhood of the building, not just your office address.
- **When:** the actual shift times and days. "Monday to Friday, 6 p.m. to 10 p.m." beats "evenings."
- **Pay:** the hourly rate. Hiding it brings in people who will leave once they find out.
- **What the work is:** offices, restrooms, floors, medical buildings, whatever it really is.

If many of your applicants speak Spanish first, post the job in Spanish too. You'll reach people who would never answer an English-only ad.

> A clear job post brings in fewer applicants, but more of the right ones. That's the trade you want.

## Call back the same day

Speed matters more than almost anything else. A good cleaner who applies on Monday may have three offers by Wednesday.

If you can't call every applicant the same day, at least send a short text:

*"Hi Maria, thanks for applying for the night cleaning job in Doral. Can you talk for 5 minutes today or tomorrow?"*

That one message tells them you're real and you're interested.

## Ask about the schedule and the drive first

Before you talk about experience, ask two questions:

1. "This shift is Monday to Friday, 6 to 10 p.m. Does that work every week, with your other job or family schedule?"
2. "The building is in [area]. How would you get there, and how long would it take?"

If either answer is shaky, it's better to find out now than after their first week. A great cleaner who can't keep your schedule isn't a great hire for this job.

## Check experience with real questions

Anyone can say they have experience. Instead of "Do you have cleaning experience?", ask questions that only someone who has done the work can answer well:

- "Walk me through how you'd clean a restroom from start to finish."
- "What do you do if you run out of supplies halfway through a shift?"
- "Have you used a floor machine? Which kind?"

You'll hear the difference quickly. People with real experience give specific, practical answers.

## Confirm every interview

A lot of interview no-shows are people who simply forgot, or weren't sure they had the time right. Two short texts fix most of that:

- **The day before:** "See you tomorrow at 10 a.m. at [address]. Reply YES to confirm."
- **That morning:** "Looking forward to meeting you at 10 today."

If someone doesn't confirm, you know to line up another interview instead of clearing your morning for nothing.

## Make the first week easy

The first few shifts decide whether a new cleaner stays. A few simple things help a lot:

- **Show them the building.** Walk through it once, with the alarm code, keys, supply closet and any tricky spots.
- **Pair them up.** If you can, have a steady team member work the first shift or two with them.
- **Check in after shift two.** A quick "How's it going? Anything you need?" catches small problems before they turn into a quit.
- **Pay on time, every time.** Nothing loses a new hire faster than a late or short first check.

## Keep a bench of good people

Some of the best candidates you meet won't be right for today's opening. Maybe the building was too far, or the shift didn't fit. Don't lose them.

Keep a simple list of good people you didn't hire, with what area and hours work for them. The next time a spot opens near them, call them first. A short list like this can save you a week of hiring.
